A LONG HUMAN STORY

Mountains have always mattered.

People have travelled through and lived among mountains for thousands of years. Mountaineering as a recreational and sporting pursuit developed gradually, shaped by exploration, science, culture and advances in equipment.

Many high peaks also hold deep spiritual and cultural significance for nearby communities. Any account of climbing history should recognize that mountaineering did not begin with European exploration, and that local knowledge has always been essential in mountain regions.

BEFORE THE MODERN ERA

Mountain knowledge and passage

Mountain communities developed routes, seasonal knowledge and practical skills for travel, trade, herding and cultural life.

18TH–19TH CENTURIES

Alpinism takes shape

Scientific curiosity, mapping and recreational climbing helped establish alpinism as a distinct pursuit, particularly in the European Alps.

1865

The Matterhorn era

The first recorded ascent of the Matterhorn by Edward Whymper's party became a defining moment in the history of Alpine climbing.

20TH CENTURY

New styles and bigger objectives

Improved equipment, expedition logistics and evolving climbing ethics expanded possibilities on rock, ice and high-altitude peaks.

TODAY

Technology and responsibility

Modern climbers combine forecasting, communication and technical equipment with growing attention to sustainability and local partnerships.
