Human achievement, changing standards and remarkable mountain history.

Mountaineering records depend on definitions, verification and context. The examples below are historical milestones rather than a live leaderboard; records and claims should always be checked against authoritative sources.

WORLD'S HIGHEST PEAK8,848.86 m

Mount Everest

The commonly accepted official height announced jointly by Nepal and China in 2020.

FIRST CONFIRMED SUMMIT1953

Everest ascent

Edmund Hillary and Tenzing Norgay reached the summit on 29 May 1953.

FIRST WINTER ASCENT1980

Everest in winter

Leszek Cichy and Krzysztof Wielicki made the first winter ascent of Everest.

ALL 14 EIGHT-THOUSANDERS14 peaks

Eight-thousander challenge

The world's 14 mountains above 8,000 metres have become a major mountaineering benchmark.

How to read a climbing record

Look for a clearly defined category, the date and route, independent verification, and whether the claim distinguishes a summit, a traverse, a speed record or a first ascent. Ethical and historical context matters as much as the headline number.
