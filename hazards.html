RISK AWARENESS

Mountains are dynamic environments.

Hazards vary with location, season, altitude, terrain and weather. A route that appears straightforward in one set of conditions may become serious in another. No checklist can remove all risk.

01

Weather and exposure

Wind, storms, low temperatures, heat and poor visibility can affect comfort, navigation and decision-making. Monitor forecasts and be prepared to retreat.

02

Avalanche terrain

Snowpack conditions can change rapidly. Use official avalanche forecasts, appropriate education and local expertise; avoid avalanche terrain when uncertainty is high.

03

Altitude-related illness

Altitude can affect people differently. Learn the warning signs through recognized mountain-medicine education, ascend conservatively and seek medical advice when needed.

04

Rockfall and falling ice

Loose rock, ice and changing temperatures can create objective hazards. Conditions and exposure should be assessed by experienced people on site.

05

Glaciers and crevasses

Glacier travel involves hidden terrain hazards and specialist rescue knowledge. It requires appropriate training, equipment and an experienced team.

06

Fatigue and dehydration

Long days, inadequate food or water and poor pacing can reduce judgement. Plan realistic objectives and respond early to signs of distress.

Emergency principle: If someone is in immediate danger, contact local emergency services or mountain rescue. Follow local rescue guidance and do not put additional people at risk.
